Renting a car in Europe should be one of the easiest travel decisions you make — the continent's motorways and back roads are superb for road trips. But between toll systems, insurance jargon and places where you simply cannot drive, first-timers get caught out. This guide covers the essentials so you can book with confidence.

## What documents do you need?

Your national driving licence is generally accepted across the EU, but an **International Driving Permit (IDP)** is recommended and legally required in some countries for non-EU licences. Check your supplier's policy before confirming — it's a simple, inexpensive document to arrange. You'll also need the same card used for booking to be presented at the desk.

## Automatic or manual?

Most rental fleets in Europe are still majority manual — automatics are limited and pricier. If you only drive automatic, filter your search to automatic-only vehicles and book early, because they sell out in summer. Don't leave this to chance at the counter.

## Tolls, vignettes and city zones

- **Italy, France, Spain and Portugal** rely heavily on toll motorways — budget for them or plan scenic free alternatives.
- **Switzerland, Austria, Slovenia, Hungary and others** require a **vignette** (windshield sticker or digital pass). Buy it before crossing the border.
- **Italy's ZTL zones** (limited-traffic areas) in city centres are fineable for entering without a permit — park outside the zone or at a guarded lot.
- **Low-emission zones** are growing in city centres across Europe; check whether your car qualifies.

## Insurance: understand the excess

European rentals always come with a basic insurance that carries an **excess** (deductible) of typically €800–€2,000. Suppliers will push you to buy their zero-excess cover at the desk — often the most expensive way to buy it. Compare the options in advance: many travellers add third-party excess protection or choose a program that includes generous cover in the quoted price.

## Cross-border rules

Tell the supplier if you plan to cross borders. Many rentals are fine within the EU, but crossing to non-EU countries can require written permission, an additional fee, and in some cases the car is not allowed at all. Never assume.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I really need an International Driving Permit?** For many non-European licences outside the EU, yes. It costs little and removes a whole category of risk — get one if there's any doubt.

**Are tolls included in the rental price?** No. Tolls are paid separately, either electronically via the car's device or at toll booths. Budget ahead for long motorway journeys.

**Can I take a rental car across European borders?** Usually within the EU with prior notice to the supplier. Non-EU destinations almost always require written permission and possibly an extra fee.
