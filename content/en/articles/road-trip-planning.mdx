A great road trip starts long before the engine does — it starts with the right vehicle matched to the right route. Rent too small and the back seats become luggage shelves. Rent too large and you're paying for fuel and parking you never needed. Here's the planning framework we use.

## Step 1: Start with the route, not the car

Mountain passes and gravel roads need different cars than motorway cruising. Coastal drives are wonderful in small convertibles; desert or mountain detours are genuinely better with more ground clearance. List your worst-case road before choosing the class — the trip will include it, even if you didn't plan it.

## Step 2: Do the luggage maths

Nobody has ever rented a car that felt too big on a road trip. Count: one large suitcase per person, a cooler or picnic bag, shopping and the inevitable "we'll just buy it here" purchases. That number of large cases decides the class more than anything else.

## Step 3: Match comfort to the driver

If you're driving five hours a day, seat comfort, cruise control and Android Auto / Apple CarPlay matter more than a sportier badge. If two drivers share the wheel, check whether the second driver is included or an extra fee awaits.

## Step 4: Know your fuel and toll budget

Renting a compact when you're doing 2,000 km of Italian or Spanish motorway will save real money on both fuel and tolls (tolls scale with class in many countries). Your budget should include a realistic fuel estimate — not just the rental headline.

## Step 5: Check breakdown essentials

For remote stretches, confirm the car comes with a spare tyre or repair kit, and that roadside assistance is part of the package. Charge cables and a paper map keyed to no-signal zones are brilliant additions.

## Frequently asked questions

**What's the best car for a European road trip?** For two people with modest luggage, a compact is the sweet spot. For four adults, step up a class — passenger comfort is the deciding factor.

**Is an SUV worth the extra cost?** Only for genuinely rough routes, big families or extreme luggage. Otherwise you're paying more in rental, fuel and tolls for comfort you won't use.

**Should I add a second driver?** If anyone else will actually drive, yes — saving a fee is not worth the risk of driving outside the rental agreement.
